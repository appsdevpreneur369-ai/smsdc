After a tooth is removed, a blood clot forms in the socket. Protecting that clot is the key to comfortable healing.

## The first 24 hours

- **Bite firmly on the gauze** for 30–45 minutes. If bleeding continues, replace it with fresh gauze and bite for another 30 minutes.
- **Don't rinse, spit forcefully or use a straw.** These can dislodge the clot.
- **Avoid smoking and alcohol.** Both slow healing and raise the risk of a painful "dry socket".
- **Rest,** and keep your head slightly raised when lying down.
- **Use a cold pack** on the cheek — 10 minutes on, 10 minutes off — to limit swelling.

## Eating and drinking

- Wait until the numbness wears off before eating, so you don't bite your cheek or tongue.
- Choose soft, lukewarm foods such as curd rice, idli, khichdi or soup. Avoid very hot, spicy or crunchy food for a few days.
- Chew on the other side.

## From the next day

- Rinse gently with warm salt water (half a teaspoon of salt in a glass of warm water) after meals.
- Keep brushing your other teeth carefully, avoiding the extraction site at first.
- Take any medicines exactly as your dentist prescribed.

## Call us if you notice

- Bleeding that doesn't slow down after an hour of firm pressure
- Pain that gets worse after two or three days
- Swelling that increases after the third day, fever, or a bad taste that won't go away
- Numbness that continues long after the procedure

Some discomfort and mild swelling are normal and usually settle within a few days.
