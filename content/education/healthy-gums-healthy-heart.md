## Did you know?

Your mouth is a window to the rest of your body. A growing body of research has **linked gum disease with heart health**. Gum disease doesn't necessarily *cause* heart problems, but the two are associated, and many of the same habits protect both.

## How gum disease may affect your heart

- **Bacteria can enter the bloodstream.** When gums are infected and inflamed, they bleed easily. Bacteria from the mouth can then pass into the bloodstream.
- **Inflammation is associated with artery problems.** Long-standing gum inflammation adds to inflammation in the body, which is associated with the build-up of plaque in the arteries.
- **Shared risk factors harm both.** Smoking and poorly controlled diabetes damage your gums *and* your heart and blood vessels.

## Warning signs of gum disease

- Gums that bleed when you brush or floss
- Red, swollen or tender gums
- Persistent bad breath
- Gums pulling away from the teeth
- Teeth that feel loose or have started to shift

Gum disease is often painless in its early stages, so regular check-ups matter even when nothing hurts.

## Simple steps to protect your gums (and your heart)

1. Brush twice a day for two minutes with a fluoride toothpaste, angling the brush towards the gum line.
2. Clean between your teeth every day with floss or interdental brushes.
3. Stop smoking or chewing tobacco — ask us for support.
4. If you have diabetes, keep your blood sugar well controlled and tell your dentist.
5. Have a professional cleaning and gum check every six months, or as advised.

## When to see us

If your gums bleed, feel swollen, or you have a heart condition or diabetes, book a gum check with our periodontist. If you take blood thinners or have a heart condition, always let us know before any dental treatment.

**Taking care of your smile helps protect your heart too!**
